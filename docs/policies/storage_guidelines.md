# Drug Storage Guidelines

**Policy Category:** Operations
**Applies to:** All Manufacturing Batches, from production through distribution
**Last Updated:** 2026-01-02

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the storage conditions required for each dosage form manufactured by Vellora Therapeutics A, to preserve product integrity between manufacturing and final distribution.

## 2. Storage Requirements by Dosage Form

| Dosage Form | Storage Condition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Injection | Cold storage (2–8°C) at all times | Required during manufacturing, QA testing, warehousing, and transport. Never store at ambient temperature, even briefly. |
| Tablet | Standard warehouse conditions (below 25°C, low humidity) | No cold chain required unless otherwise noted on the medicine record. |
| Capsule | Standard warehouse conditions (below 25°C, low humidity) | Protect from direct sunlight and moisture. |
| Syrup | Cool, dry storage (below 25°C) | Avoid temperature fluctuation; do not freeze. |

Any medicine record with a non-standard storage requirement must have this noted explicitly, and that note overrides the default table above.

## 3. Cold Chain Handling

For any batch requiring cold storage:

- The Current Location field must always reflect a designated cold-storage location (e.g. "Plant 1 - Cold Storage", "Cold Storage - Giza").
- A batch may never be recorded as sitting in a standard warehouse or production floor location for cold-chain products outside of active, time-limited handling windows.
- QA Staff performing tests on cold-chain batches (e.g. Sterility Test, pH Test) must return samples to cold storage immediately after testing.

## 4. Quarantine Storage

Batches awaiting QA disposition, or batches under investigation as part of a recall, must be physically separated from Approved stock. Quarantine locations should be clearly labeled and distinct from standard warehouse or distribution center locations (e.g. "Plant 1 - Quarantine").

## 5. Expiry Monitoring

Storage locations are responsible for flagging any batch approaching its Expiry Date. Batches nearing expiry should be prioritized for distribution ahead of newer stock (first-expiry, first-out).
